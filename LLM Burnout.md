LLMs write code and make progress on deliverables so fast it's easy to get caught up in it and not appreciate all the actual progress. Work is getting completed today in small groups of three in just weeks that would've taken an entire team months to accomplish in the past. 

With this low barrier of entry it feels compelling to have to just keep going. That if you're not actively prompting or running an agent that you are falling behind, or being a hindrance to your coworkers, or that you wont meet some arbitrary performance metric that you have in your head. 

I get so lost in prompting, in chasing the next task, because in a way it is addicting to be able to accomplish so many features, or fix so many bugs. While I may not be actively writing code, it still takes immense cognitive work to parse/guide the agents to get the work done in a manner befitting my standards. 

I struggle with this myself, but it's okay to pause. It's okay to leave a prompt unanswered, to not have to constantly be burning, constantly be thinking, constantly be writing. A day where nothing gets done, but the brain rests, is just as impactful as a day when a whole feature is shipped out the door. 

Stop. Rest. Breathe. Slow down and move with intention, everything will be waiting when you return.

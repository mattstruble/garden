It's very easy to wrongly think that everyone knows what you know, and therefore think that whatever else a person is capable of is on top of your baseline. That isn't the case, and it's really easy to get caught up in these assumptions and undervalue my own merit and intelligence. 

In general, you wouldn't be where you are in life (work, school, etc) without other people seeing value in you to be there. People wouldn't reach out to you, or trust you to do your job, unless they had inherit trust into your intelligence and skills. 

We all carry so much talent within us, so much life experience and perspective. While value is incredibly subjective, I think that just makes it more obvious to not compare your value to your peers because everyone has a role to play and not everyone needs to be the exact same. 

Similarly, imposter syndrome is a great motivator to learn more. Not to fulfill a gap, or out of guilt, but out of general curiosity for information that you are lacking and to build yourself up stronger. I think the markings of a good job is one where you always feel a bit of imposter syndrome and therefore always have something to learn.

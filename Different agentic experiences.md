In talking to other developers I've realized that they're agentic experiences (at work or personal) is vastly different than my own. 

Part of this is due to the complexities of UI/UX and the difficulties agents have with understanding UI elements and design, it's not just raw code and software design patterns, but another layer that adds on top of this. This is even more added complexity as monorepos grow and support multiple different user devices (web, desktop, android, ios). 

It's also in part by leadership and what they're trying to promote. The people who have different workstreams tend to have an "outcome driven" approach where leadership only cares about the outcome, so the developer is forced to maintain multiple agents and burn through PRs as fast as possible. When no one in power cares about the the actual thing being developed, just the outcome it creates, then it comes at ends with the developer trying to work. 

Contrasting to my experience with agents at work in which a majority of my time is spent designing, creating specs, being able to take my space and breath and ensure that what I am creating makes sense from a software architecture problem and that it actually is a net positive, instead of code for the sake of code. My day is spent doing spec driven design sessions with a single agent to create an impactful PR to drives a change, and it might be small on paper but is a step towards a much larger planned future. 

My team, manager, leads, etc, understand the complexity of code and understand that agentic coding only solves a small part of it. As part of a platform team we intentionally move slow in order to make sure that we're making the correct choices that will stand the test of time, not just quickly adapting to whatever new request comes in. In turn we have become gatekeepers (champions?) of quality, in which we're seen as people who truly care about what is being created, and truly care about the output, and therefore create truly impactful and resilient software. 

Agents can increase speeds, I personally haven't written a line of code since April of this year. However, that has been replaced with designing and ensuring that the needle is moving in the correct direction, instead of painting ourselves into a corner that we can't get out of. We still push out features in small teams that would've taken a whole org to do in months prior to agentic coding. But I think the moving slow, the moving with intention, is what still allows us to have agency and have ownership over what we create - which in turn increases our responsibility and understanding when something breaks.
